\begin{abstract}
We propose viability-preserving rollout approximate dynamic programming (ADP) for maneuver-efficient prescribed-target orbital inspection under relative-motion and safety constraints, using a directed graph of audited orbital arcs. Finite-depth lookahead assigns infinite value to actions lacking certified task-completing base-policy continuations while retaining safe zero-gain connections. Under deterministic finite-graph assumptions, exhaustive rollout preserves base-policy completion without increasing graph cost. A separate comparison of rollout depths supports retaining depth three as a balance between graph cost and computation. Using this depth, ADP completes 59/59 missions on an independently certified all-solvable development benchmark spanning three 33-view graphs, versus 57/59 for one-step ADP and 33/59 for task-aware greedy planning. An independent mixed-integer linear program (MILP) proves optima for 27 cases. Depth-three ADP has a mean relative graph-cost gap of 0.96\% across these cases and shorter median runtimes than the MILP. In a separate fixed-library confirmation, mean maneuver velocity increment decreases by 11.05\% relative to one-step-ADP-seeded local search on 43 jointly completed test missions. These results support completion-certified sequencing while distinguishing planner performance from viewpoint availability. A ROS 2 simulation tracked a fixed ADP route through twelve accepted observations, covering all nine required targets while satisfying the enabled safety checks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Orbital inspection provides visual evidence for spacecraft assessment and on-orbit servicing \cite{flores2014review}. Robot-aided inspection was demonstrated during STS-85 \cite{kimura2000inspection}, and later planning studies address large structures with one or several free-flying inspectors \cite{faghihi2022kinodynamic,faghihi2023multiple,apodaca2025trajectory}. An autonomous inspector must select observation locations and their order because relative dynamics couple transfer feasibility and maneuver effort.

Next-best-view methods model observation quality \cite{scott2003view}, kinodynamic planners combine coverage and differential constraints \cite{faghihi2022kinodynamic,faghihi2023multiple}, and relative-orbit planners exploit natural motion \cite{hou2024relative,deka2023astrodynamics}. Prescribed missions require specific targets, which an aggregate coverage threshold can miss. We therefore select candidate viewpoints and optimize their sequence to observe every required item.

The planning challenge is to reduce cumulative maneuver effort while preserving a feasible continuation to all required observations. A low-cost immediate transfer can leave costly remaining observations or exhaust the budget before completing the task. Each safe observable orbital arc (SOOA) combines a source-dependent Hill--Clohessy--Wiltshire (HCW) transfer, a stabilized camera observation, and feasibility audits. Safe connecting arcs remain useful even when they reveal no new target immediately. Fig.~\ref{fig:sooa-framework} presents the resulting workflow, from mission inputs and arc construction to rollout ADP and inspection records.

The main contribution is a viability-preserving rollout approximate dynamic programming (ADP) formulation for maneuver-efficient required-target inspection. It specializes established rollout policy improvement \cite{bertsekas1997combinatorial,bertsekas2020rollout} using complete task-aware base-policy evaluations and finite-depth Bellman expansion. Actions are evaluated by approximate total graph cost; those lacking a certified task-completing continuation receive infinite value. The analysis establishes completion preservation and base-policy no-degradation for SOOA sequencing, with exact dynamic programming providing a reference on tractable graphs.

The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a viability-preserving rollout ADP over directed SOOA graphs for maneuver-efficient required-target inspection, combining required-target terminal predicates, finite-depth Bellman improvement, complete task-aware base-policy evaluation, audited visibility and motion transitions, and safe zero-gain connectors;
  \item a finite-graph analysis proving completion preservation and base-policy cost non-degradation for repeated SOOA rollout, with exact dynamic programming providing a reference on exhaustible reduced graphs; and
  \item a reproducible evaluation of the method: an independently certified 59-case all-solvable benchmark (59/59 depth-three ADP versus 57/59 one-step ADP), a separate four-case visibility stress test, an 11.05\% mean $\Delta v$ reduction relative to one-step-ADP-seeded local search on 43 jointly completed test missions, and separate Robot Operating System 2 (ROS~2) execution checks.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\emph{Orbital inspection and sensing:} Inspection connects spacecraft observation to servicing decisions \cite{flores2014review,kimura2000inspection}. Vision-based navigation has also been tested aboard the International Space Station (ISS) with the VERTIGO/SPHERES facility \cite{tweddle2016facility}. That intravehicular microgravity testbed provides experimental sensing evidence, distinct from external structure inspection. For external missions, camera visibility and collision avoidance must be considered together: Wang et al. combine position and attitude constraints in barrier-function-based inspection trajectory generation and tracking \cite{wang2024inspection}. These requirements motivate the separation of observation acceptance, transfer admissibility, and task completion in OrbInspect.

\emph{Relative motion and inspection planning:} The Clohessy--Wiltshire equations describe relative motion near a circular chief orbit \cite{clohessy1960terminal,wertz2011space}. Williams derives inspection transfers using line-of-sight switching maneuvers \cite{williams2002inspection}; Hou et al. design relative orbits for visual inspection of the China Space Station \cite{hou2024relative}; and Zhou et al. combine near-natural relative ellipses with formation control around a rotating station \cite{zhou2026formation}. These approaches exploit orbital geometry and natural motion, which also determine the source-dependent costs and admissibility of the SOOA edges.

Robotic view planning couples sensor range, incidence, occlusion, and reconstruction quality to motion cost \cite{scott2003view}. Rapidly exploring random trees and asymptotically optimal sampling methods provide motion-search foundations \cite{lavalle1998rapidly,karaman2011sampling}. Spacecraft inspection planners extend these ideas to kinodynamic coverage \cite{faghihi2022kinodynamic,faghihi2023multiple}, receding-horizon inspection of unknown noncooperative objects \cite{maestrini2022guidance}, and astrodynamics-informed transfer sampling \cite{deka2023astrodynamics}. Recent methods further address coverage--energy trade-offs \cite{apodaca2025trajectory} and surface-aware tomographic paths \cite{zhou2026tomographic}. The present decision problem instead fixes a candidate library and prescribed target identities, then optimizes their completion sequence over audited transfers.

\emph{ADP and learning-based spacecraft control:} ADP approximates expensive exact cost-to-go calculations \cite{bertsekas1996neuro,bertsekas2020rollout}, while combinatorial rollout improves sequential decisions through heuristic completions \cite{bertsekas1997combinatorial}. Its policy-iteration interpretation connects rollout with model predictive control and terminal-set optimization \cite{bertsekas2005survey,bertsekas2017iterations}. Learning-based spacecraft applications include proximity control \cite{hu2021learning,tian2024proximity}, online attitude stabilization \cite{luo2025attitude}, reach-avoid games \cite{tang2025reachavoid}, and run-time-assured inspection \cite{vanwijk2024inspection}. OrbInspect instead uses complete model-based base-policy simulations to certify finite observation sequences.

\emph{Safety and completion guarantees:} Constrained policy optimization regulates expected constraint costs \cite{achiam2017cpo}, whereas shielding restricts action selection using a specified safety model \cite{alshiekh2018shielding}. Control barrier functions impose conditions for forward invariance \cite{ames2017barrier}, and predictive barrier functions provide a recovery mechanism toward the feasible set of a predictive safety filter \cite{wabersich2023predictive}. OrbInspect separates these control-level concerns from finite-task viability: motion audits admit SOOA edges, while finite rollout values certify a continuation to every required target. The resulting completion and cost bounds specialize established rollout principles to a directed inspection graph; they do not replace continuous-state safety certificates.

\section{System and Observation Model}
\label{sec:system}

This section defines the deterministic system, observation, and feasibility model from which the candidate graph is constructed. The inspected structure is represented by a known station mesh and surface samples; the chaser state, camera parameters, nonempty required-target set, keep-out model, and actuation limits define each scenario. These ingredients determine visibility masks, admissible transfers, and SOOA edge costs.

\subsection{Relative Dynamics}

The inspected structure is fixed in a local-vertical/local-horizontal (LVLH) frame attached to a nominal circular chief orbit. Its axes are radial, along-track, and cross-track, respectively. The chaser state is $\mathbf{x}=[\mathbf{r}^{\mathsf T},\mathbf{v}^{\mathsf T}]^{\mathsf T}\in\R^6$, where $\mathbf r,\mathbf v\in\R^3$ are the relative position and velocity. With mean motion $n$ and commanded acceleration $\mathbf u\in\R^3$, the HCW model is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \dot{\mathbf{x}}&=A_{\mathrm{HCW}}\mathbf{x}+B\mathbf{u},\\
  A_{\mathrm{HCW}}&=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_3\\K_n&C_n\end{bmatrix},
  &B&=\begin{bmatrix}0\\I_3\end{bmatrix},\\
  K_n&=\operatorname{diag}(3n^2,0,-n^2),
  &C_n&=\begin{bmatrix}0&2n&0\\-2n&0&0\\0&0&0\end{bmatrix}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:hcw-continuous}
\end{equation}
With piecewise-constant acceleration over $\dt$, fixed-step fourth-order Runge--Kutta (RK4) integration induces the linear discrete map
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}_{k+1}=\widetilde A_d\mathbf{x}_k+\widetilde B_d\mathbf{u}_k,
  \label{eq:hcw-discrete}
\end{equation}
where $\widetilde A_d\in\mathbb R^{6\times6}$ and $\widetilde B_d\in\mathbb R^{6\times3}$ are the RK4 state and input matrices associated with \eqref{eq:hcw-continuous}. The implementation evaluates the RK4 stages directly.

\subsection{Visibility, Prescribed Observation, and Coverage}

The structure is represented by indexed surface samples $\{(\mathbf p_i,\mathbf n_i,\Delta A_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$, where $\mathbf p_i\in\mathbb R^3$ is the sample position in the LVLH frame, $\mathbf n_i\in\mathbb R^3$ is its outward unit normal, and $\Delta A_i>0$ is its represented surface area. Let $\mathcal T=\{1,\ldots,N\}$ denote the sample-index set. Mission priority weights are separately defined as $w_i=\Delta A_i\eta_i$, where $\eta_i>0$ is a priority multiplier; they are not geometric areas. A fixed nonempty set $\mathcal K\subseteq\mathcal T$ specifies the required inspection items before scenario evaluation.

The camera attitude quaternion $\mathbf q_k$ specifies unit boresight $\mathbf b(\mathbf q_k)$ and the horizontal and vertical camera axes. Let $d_{i,k}^{\mathrm f}$, $d_{i,k}^{\mathrm h}$, and $d_{i,k}^{\mathrm v}$ denote the forward, horizontal, and vertical components of $\mathbf p_i-\mathbf r_k$ in this camera frame. With $\mathcal O$ denoting the occluding mesh, target $i$ is visible when $d_{i,k}^{\mathrm f}>0$ and
\begin{align}
  r_{\min}^{\mathrm{cam}}&\le\norm{\mathbf p_i-\mathbf r_k}\le r_{\max}^{\mathrm{cam}},\nonumber\\
  |d_{i,k}^{\mathrm h}|&\le d_{i,k}^{\mathrm f}\tan\alpha_{\max}^{\mathrm h},\nonumber\\
  |d_{i,k}^{\mathrm v}|&\le d_{i,k}^{\mathrm f}\tan\alpha_{\max}^{\mathrm v},\nonumber\\
  \arccos\!\frac{(\mathbf r_k-\mathbf p_i)^{\mathsf T}\mathbf n_i}
  {\norm{\mathbf r_k-\mathbf p_i}\norm{\mathbf n_i}}&\le\theta_{\max},\nonumber\\
  \los(\mathbf r_k,\mathbf p_i,\mathcal O)&=1.
  \label{eq:visibility}
\end{align}
Here $r_{\min}^{\mathrm{cam}}$ and $r_{\max}^{\mathrm{cam}}$ are the camera range limits, and $\alpha_{\max}^{\mathrm h},\alpha_{\max}^{\mathrm v}\in(0,\pi/2)$ are the horizontal and vertical FOV half-angles. The incidence-angle limit is $\theta_{\max}$, and $\los(\mathbf r_k,\mathbf p_i,\mathcal O)=1$ denotes an unobstructed LOS. Let $\mathbf m_k\in\{0,1\}^{N}$ denote the cumulative covered-target mask after the first $k$ selected observations, initialized at $\mathbf m_0=\mathbf 0$. Let $M$ denote the number of candidate observation nodes, indexed by $j=1,\ldots,M$. For a target-index set $G_j$, let $\mathbf 1_{G_j}\in\{0,1\}^{N}$ denote its indicator vector; let $\mathbf e_a\in\{0,1\}^{M}$ denote the standard basis vector that marks selected destination $a$. Fig.~\ref{fig:sooa-framework}, panel~II, illustrates the rectangular FOV and the range, incidence, and LOS tests. LOS is queried against a deterministic bounding-volume hierarchy containing every ISS mesh triangle; intersections at the target endpoint are excluded, while interior segment intersections block the view. Applying \eqref{eq:visibility} at the candidate pose $(\mathbf r_k,\mathbf q_k)=(\bar{\mathbf r}_j,\bar{\mathbf q}_j)$ yields its fixed visibility mask $G_j$. Selecting node $j$ credits this mask, so $\mathbf m_{k+1}=\mathbf m_k\lor\mathbf 1_{G_j}$. Here $\mathbf m_k=[m_{1,k},\ldots,m_{N,k}]^{\mathsf T}$, where $m_{i,k}=1$ exactly when target/sample $i$ has been credited after the first $k$ observations.
Candidate generation can make a subset of the sampled surface invisible from every candidate observation. For the nonempty set $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{insp}}=\cup_{j=1}^{M}G_j$, the priority-weighted inspectable coverage is
\begin{equation}
  C^{\mathrm{insp}}(\mathbf m_k)=
  \frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{insp}}}w_i m_{i,k}}
  {\sum_{i\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{insp}}}w_i}.
  \label{eq:inspectable-coverage}
\end{equation}
The required-target coverage fraction and the binary required-target completion indicator are defined as
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  C^{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m)&=\frac{|\mathcal K\cap\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf m)|}{|\mathcal K|},\\
  \Gamma_{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m)&=\mathbf1\{\mathcal K\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf m)\}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:required-goal}
\end{equation}
Completion denotes acceptance of the prescribed samples under \eqref{eq:visibility}. Graph-planning durations count transfers only, with exposure assumed complete at each stabilized candidate. The ROS execution durations additionally include terminal settling.

\subsection{Dynamic Feasibility and SOOAs}

Let $r_{\mathrm{veh}}$ denote the finite-body radius used to bound the chaser, and let $d_{\mathrm{safe}}$ denote the prescribed mesh-clearance margin. For an edge rollout with $K$ post-step samples, every sampled state must satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \norm{\mathbf u_k}_2\le u_{\max},\qquad
  \norm{\mathbf v_k}_2\le v_{\max},\qquad
  d_{\mathcal S}(\mathbf r_k)\ge 0,
  \label{eq:limits}
\end{equation}
where $d_{\mathcal S}(\mathbf r)=d_{\mathrm{mesh}}(\mathbf r)-r_{\mathrm{veh}}-d_{\mathrm{safe}}$ is finite-body clearance above the required margin, $d_{\mathrm{mesh}}$ is unsigned center distance to the closest triangle in the complete ISS mesh, and $d_{\mathcal S}=0$ is the sampled admissibility boundary. The mesh coordinates are obtained from the complete GL Transmission Format (glTF) scene hierarchy, followed by the Simulation Description Format (SDF) pose and scale. In addition, each consecutive pair of rollout samples is tested for a triangle-surface intersection, and its continuous center-distance lower bound subtracts half the segment length from the smaller endpoint distance by the 1-Lipschitz property of distance to a closed set. This swept test catches a direct mesh crossing even if both endpoints have positive unsigned distance.

For a directed rollout from source node $i$ to destination node $j$, the desired terminal position is $\bar{\mathbf r}_j$ and velocity is zero. Define the root-mean-square (RMS) distance to this fixed destination and the terminal position and velocity errors, respectively, as
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  e_{ij}^{\mathrm{rms}}&=\left(\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\norm{\mathbf r_{ij,k}-\bar{\mathbf r}_j}_2^2\right)^{1/2},\\
  e_{ij}^{r}&=\norm{\mathbf r_{ij,K}-\bar{\mathbf r}_j}_2,\qquad
  e_{ij}^{v}=\norm{\mathbf v_{ij,K}}_2.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:edge-errors}
\end{equation}
The RMS distance summarizes all $K$ post-step samples and contributes to the graph cost. Terminal errors use only the final sample and enter the admissibility checks.
Also define $\Delta v_{ij}=\sum_k\norm{\mathbf u_{ij,k}}_2\dt$ and $d_{ij}^{\min}=\min_k d_{\mathcal S}(\mathbf r_{ij,k})$. The fixed nonnegative weights $\lambda_v$, $\lambda_e$, $\lambda_s$, and $\lambda_a$ correspond to maneuver increment, RMS distance to the destination, clearance violation, and per-edge action cost, respectively. The graph stage cost is
\begin{equation}
  \ell_{ij}=\lambda_v\Delta v_{ij}+\lambda_e e_{ij}^{\mathrm{rms}}+\lambda_s\max(0,-d_{ij}^{\min})+\lambda_a.
  \label{eq:edge-cost}
\end{equation}
For any route, $J$ denotes the sum of its edge stage costs; $J_{\mathrm{rollout}}$ in the illustrative figure is the corresponding realized rollout cost.
For every shield-admissible edge, the clearance-violation term is zero. It remains in the shared cost implementation so deliberately unshielded comparison methods can be evaluated with the same expression.

A candidate observation node and a directed SOOA edge are, respectively,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  c_j&=(\bar{\mathbf r}_j,\bar{\mathbf q}_j,G_j),\\
  \mathsf a_{ij}&=\bigl(\mathbf x_{ij}[1{:}K],\mathbf u_{ij}[0{:}K-1],\mathbf b_{ij}[1{:}K],\\
  &\qquad \Delta v_{ij},d_{ij}^{\min},u_{ij}^{\max},v_{ij}^{\max},e_{ij}^{r},e_{ij}^{v},\rho_{ij}\bigr).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:sooa}
\end{equation}
Here $\bar{\mathbf q}_j$ is the prescribed attitude quaternion, and $G_j\subseteq\mathcal T$ is the camera-valid target set. The terminal errors $e_{ij}^{r}$ and $e_{ij}^{v}$ are defined in \eqref{eq:edge-errors}. The stored state and boresight arrays contain the $K$ post-step samples, while $u_{ij}^{\max}$ and $v_{ij}^{\max}$ record the maximum sampled input and speed norms; the source state is held by candidate node $c_i$. In the edge notation below, $i$ and $j$ index candidate nodes and are distinct from the surface-sample index used above. The initial condition is a distinguished source node $c_0$ with no visibility reward. For an optional passive audit over $L_p$ steps, zero-input HCW drift can be propagated from every stored edge state and recorded as $\rho_{ij}=\min_{1\le k\le K,\,0\le l\le L_p}d_{\mathcal S}(\mathbf r_{ij,k}^{\mathrm{drift}}(l))$. The reported study disables this optional audit and therefore makes no passive-safety claim; it retains input, speed, full-mesh sampled clearance, swept surface-intersection, and terminal checks. Throughout this paper, ``safe'' means admissible under the audits explicitly enabled for the reported experiment.

The preceding model induces the required-target sequencing problem.

\begin{problem}[Required-target SOOA planning]
\label{prob:required-target-sooa}
Given the candidate nodes and stored directed SOOA records above, an initial
action budget $H$, and the required set $\mathcal K$, find a finite sequence
$\pi=(a_1,\ldots,a_L)$, with $0\le L\le H$, that minimizes
\begin{equation}
  J(\pi)=\sum_{k=1}^{L}\ell_{j_{k-1}a_k},
  \qquad j_0=0,\quad j_k=a_k,
  \label{eq:problem-objective}
\end{equation}
subject to the following conditions: each traversed record
$\mathsf a_{j_{k-1}a_k}$ passes all enabled motion, terminal, clearance, and
passive audits; no destination is selected more than once; and the masks
updated by $\mathbf m_k=\mathbf m_{k-1}\lor\mathbf 1_{G_{a_k}}$, $\mathbf m_0=\mathbf0$, satisfy
$\Gamma_{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m_L)=1$. A zero-gain action is allowed when it is needed
to reach a later required observation. If no such sequence exists within $H$,
the instance is declared infeasible in the available candidate graph.
\end{problem}

\section{Viability-Preserving Rollout ADP}
\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{Candidate Graph and Markov State}

The candidate graph contains the initial node $c_0$ and the $M$ candidate observation nodes $c_1,\ldots,c_M$ in \eqref{eq:sooa}. A directed edge from $c_i$ to $c_j$ is the source-dependent SOOA $\mathsf a_{ij}$, and it is absent when an enabled audit fails. Thus visibility $G_j$ is node dependent, whereas trajectory, cost, clearance, input, terminal error, and passive margin are edge dependent. This separation is necessary because reaching the same observation from two different source nodes generally produces different HCW motion and audit values.

A node identifier is not a sufficient decision state because the value of a view changes after its targets have been covered. The finite-horizon Markov state is
\begin{equation}
  s_k=(j_k,\mathbf m_k,\boldsymbol\beta_k,h_k),
  \label{eq:mdp-state}
\end{equation}
where $j_k\in\{0,\ldots,M\}$ is the current node, $\mathbf m_k\in\{0,1\}^{N}$ is the covered-target mask, $\boldsymbol\beta_k\in\{0,1\}^{M}$ is the selected-observation mask, and $h_k$ is the remaining action budget. Selecting action $a\in\{1,\ldots,M\}$ chooses destination node $c_a$, traverses edge $\mathsf a_{j_ka}$, and gives the deterministic transition
\begin{equation}
  f(s_k,a)=\left(a,\;\mathbf m_k\lor\mathbf 1_{G_a},\;\boldsymbol\beta_k\lor\mathbf e_a,\;h_k-1\right).
  \label{eq:mdp-transition}
\end{equation}
The required set $\mathcal K$ is fixed problem data. The shield-admissible action set $\mathcal U_s(s)$ is defined later. The constrained graph problem minimizes total stage cost subject to \eqref{eq:required-goal}, with zero terminal cost for a completed task and value $+\infty$ when no admissible completion exists within the remaining budget. Its Bellman recursion is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  V^\star(s)&=
    \begin{cases}
    0, & \Gamma_{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m)=1,\\
    +\infty, & h=0\ \text{or}\ \mathcal U_s(s)=\varnothing,\\
    \min\limits_{a\in\mathcal U_s(s)}
      Q^\star(s,a), & \text{otherwise},
    \end{cases}\\
  Q^\star(s,a)&=\ell_{ja}+V^\star(f(s,a)).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:bellman}
\end{equation}
Here $j$ and $h$ are the current-node and remaining-budget components of $s$, and $f$ is the deterministic state-transition map. Exact evaluation is exponential in the coverage and selected-node masks. The proposed method approximates this recursion with a short exact prefix and a computationally inexpensive, goal-aware terminal policy.

\subsection{SOOA Construction and Safety Shield}

Area-weighted targets are sampled from the mesh, and camera seeds are generated on offset shells around their outward normals. Each fixed visibility mask $G_j$ is computed once from the terminal observation geometry. For a transition to $\bar{\mathbf x}_j=[\bar{\mathbf r}_j^{\mathsf T},\mathbf0^{\mathsf T}]^{\mathsf T}$, the planner solves the minimum-energy piecewise-constant HCW boundary-value problem under the RK4 map in \eqref{eq:hcw-discrete}:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \min_{\mathbf u_0,\ldots,\mathbf u_{K-1}}\quad&
  \sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\norm{\mathbf u_k}_2^2\\
  \mathrm{s.t.}\quad&
  \mathbf x_{k+1}=\widetilde A_d\mathbf x_k+\widetilde B_d\mathbf u_k,\\
  &\mathbf x_K=\bar{\mathbf x}_j.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:terminal-transfer}
\end{equation}
If the terminal influence matrix $\mathcal B_K=[\widetilde A_d^{K-1}\widetilde B_d\ \cdots\ \widetilde B_d]\in\mathbb R^{6\times3K}$ has full row rank, stack the commands as $\mathbf U_{ij}=[\mathbf u_{ij,0}^{\mathsf T},\ldots,\mathbf u_{ij,K-1}^{\mathsf T}]^{\mathsf T}$. The minimum-norm command from source state $\mathbf x_i^{\mathrm{src}}$ is then $\mathbf U_{ij}^\star=\mathcal B_K^{\mathsf T}(\mathcal B_K\mathcal B_K^{\mathsf T})^{-1}(\bar{\mathbf x}_j-\widetilde A_d^K\mathbf x_i^{\mathrm{src}})$. The implementation computes this expression through the $6\times6$ Gram system and then propagates the requested input without clipping. Let $\varepsilon_r$ and $\varepsilon_v$ denote the terminal position and velocity tolerances, respectively. Then $\chi_{ij}=1$ exactly when every stored edge sample satisfies \eqref{eq:limits}, the enabled swept-segment audit reports no triangle intersection, $e_{ij}^{r}\le\varepsilon_r$, $e_{ij}^{v}\le\varepsilon_v$, and $\rho_{ij}\ge0$ when the passive audit is enabled. The shield admits
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal U_s(s)=\{a\in\{1,\ldots,M\}:h>0,\;\beta_a=0,\;\chi_{ja}=1\},
  \label{eq:safe-action-set}
\end{equation}
where $j$ is the current-node component of $s$. Safe unvisited nodes remain admissible when they provide no immediate inspection gain, because a connecting action may be needed to reach a required target on the directed graph. The finite budget and no-revisit mask bound such connections. The shield makes an inadmissible edge unavailable to incumbent construction, local search, and exact recursion \cite{alshiekh2018shielding}. Its guarantee remains conditional on the sampled numerical rollout and deterministic model used to compute $\chi_{ij}$.

An edge is audit-admissible when $\chi_{ij}=1$; an action is shield-admissible when it belongs to $\mathcal U_s(s)$. A scenario is feasible for the candidate graph when at least one shield-admissible sequence reaches $\Gamma_{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m)=1$ within the available budget, and is infeasible when no such sequence exists.

\subsection{Adaptive Base Policy and Rollout Value}

Let $g_{\mathcal K}(s,a)=|\mathcal K\cap(G_a\setminus\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf m))|$ count newly accepted required targets, and let $\epsilon>0$ prevent division by a near-zero stage cost. For the required-target task, the deterministic adaptive base policy is
\begin{equation}
  \mu(s)\in\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal U_s(s)}
  \left(\frac{g_{\mathcal K}(s,a)}{\max\{\epsilon,\ell_{ja}\}},
  g_{\mathcal K}(s,a),-\ell_{ja}\right),
  \label{eq:base-policy}
\end{equation}
where the tuple is compared lexicographically, followed by deterministic node-identifier tie breaking. The policy recomputes gain after every accepted observation; zero-gain ties favor lower stage cost. Starting from state $s$, its completion value is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat V_0(s)=
  \begin{cases}
  \sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\ell_{j_k\mu(s_k)}, &
  \begin{aligned}[t]
    &\mu\text{ reaches}\\
    &\Gamma_{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m)=1\text{ within }h,
  \end{aligned}\\[2pt]
  +\infty, & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:base-value}
\end{equation}
where $L$ is the number of actions taken by the base policy before completion or budget exhaustion. Thus $\widehat V_0$ approximates the cost-to-go through a complete, shielded base-policy simulation.

For rollout depth $d\ge1$, define the truncated Bellman value
\begin{equation}
  \widehat V_d(s)=
  \begin{cases}
    0, & \Gamma_{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m)=1,\\
    +\infty, & h=0,\\
    \min\limits_{a\in\mathcal U_s(s)}\widehat Q_d(s,a), & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:rollout-value}
\end{equation}
where $\widehat Q_d(s,a)=\ell_{ja}+\widehat V_{d-1}(f(s,a))$.
The minimum over an empty action set is $+\infty$. The policy selects a finite-valued minimizing action, applies \eqref{eq:mdp-transition}, and rebuilds the depth-$d$ tree at the successor state. State--depth memoization removes duplicate subproblems within each decision. The guarantees below require exhaustive prefix evaluation and the same deterministic Markov base policy at every leaf.
Alg.~\ref{alg:safe-graph-api} summarizes this finite-value action selection and repeated replanning procedure.
Fig.~\ref{fig:adp-architecture} gives a worked decision on an illustrative graph, separating motion admissibility from completion certification and showing the continuation retained after replanning.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Viability-preserving rollout ADP}
\label{alg:safe-graph-api}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Candidate graph, shield $\mathcal U_s$, required set $\mathcal K$, budget $H$, depth $d$
\State $s\gets(0,\mathbf0,\mathbf0,H)$; $\pi\gets()$ \Comment{$\pi$ stores the selected action sequence}
\While{$\Gamma_{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m)=0$ and $h>0$}
  \State Enumerate every $a\in\mathcal U_s(s)$
  \For{each admissible $a$}
    \State $\widehat Q_d(s,a)\gets\ell_{ja}+\widehat V_{d-1}(f(s,a))$
  \EndFor
  \State Discard every action with $\widehat Q_d(s,a)=+\infty$
  \If{no finite action remains} \State \Return no certified completion \EndIf
  \State $a^\star\gets\arg\min_a \widehat Q_d(s,a)$ with deterministic ties
  \State Append $a^\star$ to $\pi$
  \State $s\gets f(s,a^\star)$
\EndWhile
\State \Return $\pi$ if $\Gamma_{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m)=1$; otherwise return no certified completion
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Rollout Guarantees and Exact Oracle}
\label{sec:theory}

For fixed masks and nonnegative weights, $F(S)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_i\mathbf1\{i\in\cup_{j\in S}G_j\}$ is normalized, monotone, and submodular \cite{nemhauser1978submodular}. Unit weights on $\mathcal K$ give the same diminishing-returns property for required-target gain. This coverage fact neither justifies removing zero-gain connectors nor supplies a greedy approximation ratio, because SOOA cost and next-action admissibility depend on the current node.

Under the fixed deterministic graph assumptions, the state in \eqref{eq:mdp-state} is Markov: $j_k$ identifies the source-dependent edges, $\mathbf m_k$ determines unmet requirements, $\boldsymbol\beta_k$ enforces no revisits, and $h_k$ gives the remaining budget. Partial dwell, independent look directions, or time-varying illumination would require additional state. If idealized rest-to-rest endpoints match exactly, concatenating edges with $\chi_{ij}=1$ also preserves their recorded input, speed, sampled-clearance, swept-intersection, terminal, and enabled passive-drift results.

\begin{theorem}[Completion and base-policy bound]
\label{thm:rollout-guarantees}
Consider the deterministic finite graph, exhaustive depth-$d$ prefix evaluation, and the same deterministic Markov base policy at every leaf. For fixed $d\ge1$: (i) if $\widehat V_d(s)<+\infty$, repeated replanning in Algorithm~\ref{alg:safe-graph-api} reaches the goal through shield-admissible actions within the remaining budget; and (ii) if $\widehat V_0(s)<+\infty$, then the realized graph cost satisfies $J_{\mathrm{rollout}}\le\widehat V_d(s)\le\widehat V_0(s)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Finiteness of $\widehat V_d(s)$ provides a shield-admissible depth-$d$ prefix followed by a base-policy completion. After the first action, the retained tail, extended when necessary by the next base-policy action, remains a candidate in the rebuilt depth-$d$ tree. The rebuilt value at the successor is therefore finite, and induction over the decreasing budget proves completion. If $\widehat V_0(s)$ is finite, the first $d$ base-policy actions followed by the remaining base completion are feasible in \eqref{eq:rollout-value}, so $\widehat V_d(s)\le\widehat V_0(s)$. More generally, whenever $\widehat V_{d-1}(s')<+\infty$, the same certificate can be extended by the next base-policy action when needed (or terminates earlier at the goal), giving $\widehat V_d(s')\le\widehat V_{d-1}(s')$. Therefore, for a minimizing first action $a$ and successor $s'$, $\ell_{ja}+\widehat V_d(s')\le\ell_{ja}+\widehat V_{d-1}(s')=\widehat V_d(s)$. Summing over replans yields $J_{\mathrm{rollout}}\le\widehat V_d(s)$. These bounds concern graph stage cost, not physical $\Delta v$ alone.
\end{IEEEproof}

For reduced-graph certification, exact recursion caches \eqref{eq:bellman} for every reachable tuple $(j,\mathbf m,\boldsymbol\beta,h)$ and enumerates every action in $\mathcal U_s(s)$. Exhaustive evaluation returns a minimum-cost task-completing sequence or $+\infty$ if none exists in the fixed sampled graph; a finite-horizon induction proof is included in the supplementary material. This result does not cover omitted camera poses or continuous-space trajectories.

With maximum branching factor $B_{\max}$ and at most $H$ base-policy actions, one depth-$d$ decision evaluation costs $O(HB_{\max}^{d+1})$ in the worst case; at most $H$ replans give a direct whole-route bound of $O(H^2B_{\max}^{d+1})$. Memoization reduces repeated evaluation when prefixes reach the same mask state. The Results section and Supplementary Table~S2 quantify the cost--computation trade-off for $d=1,\ldots,6$.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:simulation}

\subsection{Fixed Geometry and Required-Target Profiles}

The primary study uses the public ISS mesh distributed by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) \cite{nasa_iss_gltf_model}. Its complete transformed geometry contains $247\,525$ triangles. 90 equal-represented-area samples define the original surface denominator; 41 are observable from at least one of the 24 retained candidate observations. The directed archive contains 24 initial-to-observation and $24\times23$ inter-observation records, of which 264 pass every enabled gate. Every method receives the same graph and scenario-specific available-node set.

Required-target profiles are selected deterministically before scenario evaluation by centroid-first, normalized farthest-point sampling within three equal-width mesh slabs. The primary nine-target profile assigns three samples to each slab; Supplementary Table~S1 gives the complete 6-, 9-, and 12-target inventories. These synthetic profiles are not an operational ISS maintenance checklist.

The required set remains fixed when a scenario removes observation nodes. Losing every available view of a required item makes the task impossible in the library. Otherwise, a valid route or exhausted exact search establishes feasibility status; cases without either certificate remain unresolved.

\subsection{Dynamics, Costs, and Observation Accounting}

Each rest-to-rest SOOA lasts \unit{90}{s} and uses fixed-step RK4 propagation with $\dt=\unit{3}{s}$. The HCW mean motion is $1.13137\times10^{-3}~\mathrm{rad/s}$, the acceleration limit is $u_{\max}=0.060~\mathrm{m/s^2}$, and the speed limit is $v_{\max}=\unit{2.0}{m/s}$. The finite-body radius is $r_{\mathrm{veh}}=\unit{0.80}{m}$, which conservatively bounds the chaser visual model, and the prescribed full-mesh clearance margin is $d_{\mathrm{safe}}=\unit{2.0}{m}$. Terminal position and velocity tolerances are \unit{0.5}{m} and \unit{0.05}{m/s}. For the reported simulations, we set $\lambda_{v}=5$, $\lambda_{e}=0.18$, $\lambda_{s}=120$, and $\lambda_{a}=0.05$, weighting maneuver increment, RMS distance to the destination, clearance violation, and per-edge action cost, respectively, in \eqref{eq:edge-cost}. The base-policy denominator floor is $\epsilon=0.05$, and the action budget is 14. Required-only completion has no additional global area threshold.

Candidate visibility uses a rectangular $70^{\circ}\times50^{\circ}$ FOV (horizontal $\times$ vertical), giving $\alpha_{\max}^{\mathrm h}=35^{\circ}$ and $\alpha_{\max}^{\mathrm v}=25^{\circ}$ in \eqref{eq:visibility}. The camera range is $2$--$54~\mathrm{m}$ and the maximum incidence angle is $60^{\circ}$; full-mesh LOS is checked separately. Target-identity unions exclude duplicate credit. Reporting includes required-target completion $C^{\mathcal K}$, priority-weighted inspectable score $C^{\mathrm{insp}}$, and unweighted fractions over the 41 observable and 90 original samples. Equal represented sample areas make the last fraction the sampled-area estimate; priority multipliers affect only the weighted score.

\subsection{Study Chronology and Fresh Confirmation}

An exploratory campaign preceded the confirmation. It evaluated three fixed profiles without feasibility rejection; its outcomes are reported as development evidence in Supplementary Table~S3. The campaign motivated the fresh comparator described below, but it is not part of the main comparison.

A fresh confirmation compares depth-three ADP with one-step ADP and local search initialized by a completed one-step ADP route. Methods, penalties, scenario identities, and implementation hashes were frozen before evaluating the new outcomes. The confirmation retains the nine prescribed targets and depth-three policy, with \RTValidationN{} validation, \RTTestN{} test, and \RTOodN{} shifted scenarios. Its seeds are disjoint from all development runs, and every generated case is retained.

Each scenario samples a node-removal probability uniformly from $[0,0.10]$, then removes candidates independently using that probability. It multiplies priorities for approximately 6\%--14\% of targets by a factor in $[2,4]$. Shifted scenarios use removal probabilities in $[0.15,0.25]$, priority fractions of 12\%--20\%, and factors in $[5,8]$. Priorities affect background scores and the coverage-only ablation, but not the required-target goal or stage cost. All scenarios perturb one frozen graph, and distinct seeds may yield identical routes.

After the fixed-library confirmation, a separate depth diagnostic evaluated $d=1,\ldots,6$ on its 12 validation scenarios, distinct from the test and shifted sets. Depth three was already fixed for that confirmation, whose results were unchanged. The diagnostic supported retaining this setting for the subsequent all-solvable benchmark. Its outcomes are presented first in Section~\ref{sec:depth-diagnostic} and Supplementary Table~S2.

\subsection{Matched Baselines and Statistical Reporting}

The confirmation includes the task-aware greedy incumbent, one-step ADP, ADP-seeded local search, and depth-three ADP under identical tasks, dynamics, safety gates, and action budgets. One-step ADP uses \eqref{eq:rollout-value} with $d=1$. Local search applies three audited best-improvement reversal passes to its completed route and retains the feasible seed if improvement fails. This comparator improves an ADP-initialized ordering; it does not introduce new viewpoints. Depth-three ADP uses the original greedy base value without a one-step fallback.

A coverage-only ablation retains the earlier 80\% priority-weighted stopping rule and is evaluated against the same required-target identities. It tests the mission objective separately from the matched complete-task cost comparison.

Success and missing-target counts use every scenario; graph cost and physical $\Delta v$ use jointly completed ADP/seeded-local pairs. For a route with final covered-target mask $\mathbf m$, the all-scenario score is $J_{\mathrm{pen}}=J+500(1-C^{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m))+500(1-\Gamma_{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m))$, with fixed evaluation penalties distinct from physical maneuver cost. Paired-mean percentile intervals use $10\,000$ resamples, and exact two-sided sign tests exclude numerical ties. Timing includes initialization, including the one-step seed for local search; safety-screen counts cover the selected planner call.

\subsection{All-Solvable Redundant-Viewpoint Benchmark}
\label{sec:redundant-view-protocol}

After the depth diagnostic, a redundant-viewpoint pilot tested whether additional viewpoints reduce the visibility bottleneck while permitting independent route certification. Three frozen seeds generated 33-view graphs on the same mesh, with the same nine required targets, initial state, camera model, dynamics, costs, and safety limits. Each graph contained three separated views per required target and six connecting views anchored to other observable samples. Candidate positions were sampled \unit{26--42}{m} from their source target within a $35^{\circ}$ cone around its surface normal, with the camera aimed at that target. From 64 attempts per source, deterministic first-valid and farthest-point selection retained geometry-valid views separated by at least \unit{4}{m}. All rejected attempts were archived. Each graph's $33+33\times32=1\,089$ directed transfers were regenerated and audited against the full mesh.

Each graph supplied one reference case, ten nominal cases and ten shifted cases, using the confirmation's node-removal probability ranges. All 63 cases were retained. The 59 cases with an independent feasible certificate define the primary all-solvable benchmark (three reference, 30 nominal and 26 shifted cases). The four remaining shifted cases are retained as a visibility-loss stress test and are excluded only from the primary solvable-case denominator. Background weights were fixed because they do not affect required-only completion or graph cost. The generation rules, seeds and source hashes were frozen before graph construction. This pilot followed inspection of the confirmation failures and is development evidence; it does not replace the frozen confirmation.

An independent mixed-integer linear program (MILP) served as an offline finite-graph reference and feasibility certificate. Binary arc and node variables enforced flow, required-target coverage, no revisits and the 14-observation budget; increasing node ranks excluded disconnected cycles. Its objective was the same additive graph cost, including the 0.05 action charge. A reconstructed valid route certified feasibility. Missing required visibility or a solver infeasibility result certified impossibility in the available graph; a time limit without a valid route remained unresolved. We labelled a case proven optimal only when HiGHS supplied an optimality proof and the reconstructed route cost matched its branch-and-bound lower bound within numerical tolerance. A time-limited feasible route was retained as a checked incumbent, not an optimum. Certification preceded ADP evaluation and supplied no route to either ADP depth.

Greedy construction, one-step ADP, depth-three ADP and the independent MILP each received a nominal \unit{15}{s} budget, including initialization or model construction and excluding shared graph generation. The pilot used Python~3.12.3 and SciPy~1.11.4/HiGHS on Ubuntu. Each method--case pair was timed once; greedy/ADP order was shuffled, while MILP timing came from the preceding certification phase. The MILP timing quantifies the computational cost of the finite-graph reference search; the solver was not used to initialize or constrain ADP. Native solver termination could slightly exceed its requested limit. The primary success-rate denominator is the independently certified all-solvable benchmark; all 63 generated cases and the four stress cases remain available for transparent audit. Cost gaps used only cases with proven optima.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results support model-based rollout ADP for prescribed-target inspection: a base-policy completion supplies a checkable continuation, and Bellman lookahead improves the next action. Required targets determine completion, while an optional background threshold can be added through the same terminal predicate. This sequencing layer complements learning-based proximity control \cite{hu2021learning,tian2024proximity,vanwijk2024inspection}. Failure to obtain a finite rollout value does not prove graph infeasibility, because the base policy may miss a feasible continuation.

The fixed-library comparison measures maneuver savings against one-step ADP and seeded reversal search. The independent MILP is best interpreted as an exact finite-graph reference and feasibility certificate, rather than as the proposed deployment planner. It supplies a cost floor when solved to optimality, but its 15-s budget left 32 feasible cases without an optimality proof and its median runtimes exceeded those of depth-three ADP. The proposed method therefore targets the speed-quality trade-off: it matched MILP completion on the 59 certified-solvable cases. Its mean relative graph-cost gap was 0.96\% across the 27 cases with proven optima. Because the pilot uses a new viewpoint library and dependent cases, it cannot be paired with the original campaign; repeated timing and a larger frozen confirmation are still needed.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper developed viability-preserving rollout ADP for prescribed-target inspection over audited orbital arcs. It completed every feasible fixed-library confirmation case and reduced mean $\Delta v$ by 11.05\% relative to seeded local search on 43 jointly completed test missions. In the redundant-viewpoint development benchmark, depth-three ADP completed all 59 cases independently certified as solvable by the MILP, with shorter median runtimes than the MILP. Its mean relative graph-cost gap was 0.96\% across the 27 cases with proven optima. These results support completion-certified sequencing as a computationally lighter alternative to exact finite-graph search, within the stated graph and audit assumptions, and motivate a larger frozen evaluation of viewpoint redundancy.
