\begin{abstract}
Autonomous orbital inspection must limit maneuver effort while completing prescribed observations under relative-motion and safety constraints. This paper develops a viability-preserving rollout approximate dynamic programming (ADP) formulation on a directed graph of audited orbital arcs. A finite Bellman prefix uses a deterministic task-aware base policy's completion cost as its approximate terminal value. Uncertified completions receive infinite value, while safe connecting actions remain available without immediate inspection gain. Under deterministic finite-graph assumptions, exhaustive rollout preserves completion and does not exceed the graph cost of a completing base policy. To separate planner failure from unavailable viewpoints, we independently certify route feasibility before evaluating the planners. In a development pilot, this defines a primary, feasibility-conditioned all-solvable benchmark of 59 cases from three 33-view graphs: depth-three ADP completes 59/59 missions, compared with 57/59 for one-step ADP and 33/59 for the task-aware greedy policy. Its mean graph-cost gap is 0.96\% over 27 cases with a proven finite-graph optimum. Four additional shifted cases failing the visibility certificate are retained as a separate stress test. The original fixed-library confirmation is reported separately: depth-three ADP completes \RTTestSuccess/\RTTestN{} test and \RTOodSuccess/\RTOodN{} shifted missions, and reduces mean maneuver velocity increment, $\Delta v$, by 11.05\% on 43 jointly completed test missions relative to one-step-ADP-seeded local search. These results support completion-certified sequencing while identifying viewpoint availability as a separate mission-level constraint.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Orbital inspection provides visual evidence for assessing spacecraft condition and planning on-orbit servicing \cite{flores2014review}. Robot-aided inspection and flaw detection were demonstrated during STS-85 \cite{kimura2000inspection}, and subsequent planning studies address coverage of large structures with one or several free-flying inspectors \cite{faghihi2022kinodynamic,faghihi2023multiple,apodaca2025trajectory}. An autonomous inspector must decide where to observe and how to reach each observation. Relative dynamics couple these decisions because viewpoint order changes transfer feasibility and cumulative maneuver effort.

Next-best-view methods model observation quality \cite{scott2003view}, kinodynamic planners combine coverage and differential constraints \cite{faghihi2022kinodynamic,faghihi2023multiple}, and relative-orbit planners exploit natural motion \cite{hou2024relative,deka2023astrodynamics}. A prescribed inspection mission also distinguishes mandatory surface targets from optional background coverage. An aggregate threshold can be reached while a required target remains unobserved. We therefore fix target identities and optimize the viewpoints and sequence needed to observe every prescribed item.

The planning challenge is to reduce cumulative maneuver effort while preserving a feasible continuation to all required observations. A low-cost immediate transfer can leave costly remaining observations or exhaust the budget before completing the task. Each safe observable orbital arc (SOOA) combines a source-dependent Hill--Clohessy--Wiltshire (HCW) transfer, a stabilized camera observation, and feasibility audits. Safe connecting arcs remain useful even when they reveal no new target immediately. Fig.~\ref{fig:sooa-framework} presents the resulting workflow, from mission inputs and arc construction to rollout ADP and inspection records.

The main contribution is a viability-preserving rollout approximate dynamic programming (ADP) formulation for maneuver-efficient required-target inspection. It specializes established rollout policy improvement \cite{bertsekas1997combinatorial,bertsekas2020rollout} using complete task-aware base-policy evaluations and finite-depth Bellman expansion. Actions are evaluated by approximate total graph cost, with infinite values rejecting uncertified completions. The analysis establishes completion preservation and base-policy no-degradation for SOOA sequencing, with exact dynamic programming providing a reference on tractable graphs.

The contributions are:
\begin{samepage}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a viability-preserving rollout ADP over directed SOOA graphs for maneuver-efficient required-target inspection, combining required-target terminal predicates, finite-depth Bellman improvement, complete task-aware base-policy evaluation, audited visibility and motion transitions, and safe zero-gain connectors;
  \item a finite-graph analysis establishing Markov sufficiency, specializing rollout completion and cost-improvement guarantees to SOOA sequencing, and proving exact optimality on exhausted reduced graphs; and
  \item a reproducible evaluation of the method: an independently certified 59-case all-solvable benchmark (59/59 depth-three ADP versus 57/59 one-step ADP), a separate four-case visibility stress test, an 11.05\% mean $\Delta v$ reduction relative to one-step-ADP-seeded local search on 43 jointly completed test missions, and separate Robot Operating System 2 (ROS~2) execution checks.
\end{enumerate}
\end{samepage}


\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\emph{Orbital inspection and sensing:} Inspection connects spacecraft observation to servicing decisions \cite{flores2014review,kimura2000inspection}. Vision-based navigation has also been tested aboard the International Space Station (ISS) with the VERTIGO/SPHERES facility \cite{tweddle2016facility}. That intravehicular microgravity testbed provides experimental sensing evidence, distinct from external structure inspection. For external missions, camera visibility and collision avoidance must be considered together: Wang et al. combine position and attitude constraints in barrier-function-based inspection trajectory generation and tracking \cite{wang2024inspection}. These requirements motivate the separation of observation acceptance, transfer admissibility, and task completion in OrbInspect.

\emph{Relative motion and inspection planning:} The Clohessy--Wiltshire equations describe relative motion near a circular chief orbit \cite{clohessy1960terminal,wertz2011space}. Williams derives inspection transfers using line-of-sight switching maneuvers \cite{williams2002inspection}; Hou et al. design relative orbits for visual inspection of the China Space Station \cite{hou2024relative}; and Zhou et al. combine near-natural relative ellipses with formation control around a rotating station \cite{zhou2026formation}. These approaches exploit orbital geometry and natural motion, which also determine the source-dependent costs and admissibility of the SOOA edges.

Robotic view planning couples sensor range, incidence, occlusion, and reconstruction quality to motion cost \cite{scott2003view}. Rapidly exploring random trees and asymptotically optimal sampling methods provide motion-search foundations \cite{lavalle1998rapidly,karaman2011sampling}. Spacecraft inspection planners extend these ideas to kinodynamic coverage \cite{faghihi2022kinodynamic,faghihi2023multiple}, receding-horizon inspection of unknown noncooperative objects \cite{maestrini2022guidance}, and astrodynamics-informed transfer sampling \cite{deka2023astrodynamics}. Recent methods further address coverage--energy trade-offs \cite{apodaca2025trajectory} and surface-aware tomographic paths \cite{zhou2026tomographic}. The present decision problem instead fixes a candidate library and prescribed target identities, then optimizes their completion sequence over audited transfers.

\emph{ADP and learning-based spacecraft control:} ADP replaces expensive exact cost-to-go calculations with tractable approximations \cite{bertsekas1996neuro,bertsekas2020rollout}. Combinatorial rollout uses heuristic completions to improve sequential decisions \cite{bertsekas1997combinatorial}; its policy-iteration interpretation also connects rollout with model predictive control \cite{bertsekas2005survey}. Value and policy iteration provide a broader theoretical basis for terminal-set optimal control \cite{bertsekas2017iterations}. OrbInspect uses a complete base-policy simulation as its value approximation, rather than a learned critic.

Reinforcement learning offers a complementary route to approximate optimal feedback control from trajectory data \cite{kiumarsi2018survey}. Spacecraft applications include constrained six-degree-of-freedom proximity control \cite{hu2021learning}, online attitude stabilization under dynamic uncertainty \cite{luo2025attitude}, and actor--critic ADP with motion constraints, input saturation, and disturbance compensation \cite{tian2024proximity}. Reach-avoid differential graphical games provide a related ADP setting for nonlinear multi-agent dynamics with communication constraints and input saturation \cite{tang2025reachavoid}; their adversarial objectives differ from the deterministic required-observation sequencing studied here. For inspection specifically, van Wijk et al. combine deep reinforcement learning (DRL) with discrete control barrier functions and run-time assurance \cite{vanwijk2024inspection}. These studies concern learned control policies; the present model-based ADP contribution concerns completion-certified observation sequencing.

\emph{Safety and completion guarantees:} Constrained policy optimization regulates expected constraint costs \cite{achiam2017cpo}, whereas shielding restricts action selection using a specified safety model \cite{alshiekh2018shielding}. Control barrier functions impose conditions for forward invariance \cite{ames2017barrier}, and predictive barrier functions provide a recovery mechanism toward the feasible set of a predictive safety filter \cite{wabersich2023predictive}. OrbInspect separates these control-level concerns from finite-task viability: motion audits admit SOOA edges, while finite rollout values certify a continuation to every required target. The resulting completion and cost bounds specialize established rollout principles to a directed inspection graph; they do not replace continuous-state safety certificates.

\section{System and Observation Model}
\label{sec:system}

This section defines the deterministic system, observation, and feasibility model from which the candidate graph is constructed. The inspected structure is represented by a known station mesh and surface samples; the chaser state, camera parameters, nonempty required-target set, keep-out model, and actuation limits define each scenario. These ingredients determine visibility masks, admissible transfers, and SOOA edge costs.

\subsection{Relative Dynamics}

The inspected structure is fixed in a local-vertical/local-horizontal (LVLH) frame attached to a nominal circular chief orbit. Its axes are radial, along-track, and cross-track, respectively. The chaser state is $\mathbf{x}=[\mathbf{r}^{\mathsf T},\mathbf{v}^{\mathsf T}]^{\mathsf T}\in\R^6$, where $\mathbf r=[r_x,r_y,r_z]^{\mathsf T}$ and $\mathbf v=[v_x,v_y,v_z]^{\mathsf T}$. With mean motion $n$ and commanded acceleration $\mathbf{u}=[a_x,a_y,a_z]^{\mathsf T}$, the HCW model is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \dot{\mathbf{x}}&=A_{\mathrm{HCW}}\mathbf{x}+B\mathbf{u},\\
  A_{\mathrm{HCW}}&=\begin{bmatrix}0&I\\K_n&C_n\end{bmatrix},
  &B&=\begin{bmatrix}0\\I\end{bmatrix},\\
  K_n&=\operatorname{diag}(3n^2,0,-n^2),
  &C_n&=\begin{bmatrix}0&2n&0\\-2n&0&0\\0&0&0\end{bmatrix}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:hcw-continuous}
\end{equation}
where $I$ is the $3\times3$ identity matrix and the zero blocks have compatible dimensions.
For the continuous HCW model, piecewise-constant acceleration over $\dt$ has the exact zero-order-hold map
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \mathbf{x}_{k+1}&=A_d\mathbf{x}_k+B_d\mathbf{u}_k,\\
  A_d&=e^{A_{\mathrm{HCW}}\dt},\\
  B_d&=\int_0^{\dt}e^{A_{\mathrm{HCW}}\tau}B\,d\tau.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:hcw-discrete}
\end{equation}
% The implementation constructs and propagates each boundary map with fixed-step fourth-order Runge--Kutta integration under the same piecewise-constant input model. The numerical map is $\mathbf x_{k+1}=\widetilde A_d\mathbf x_k+\widetilde B_d\mathbf u_k$, with \eqref{eq:hcw-discrete} providing the exact continuous-model reference.

\subsection{Visibility, Prescribed Observation, and Coverage}

The structure is represented by indexed surface samples $\{(\mathbf p_i,\mathbf n_i,a_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$, where $\mathbf p_i\in\mathbb R^3$ is the sample position in the LVLH frame, $\mathbf n_i\in\mathbb R^3$ is its outward unit normal, and $a_i>0$ is its represented surface area. Let $\mathcal T=\{1,\ldots,N\}$ denote the sample-index set. Mission priority weights are separately defined as $w_i=a_i\eta_i$, where $\eta_i>0$ is a priority multiplier; they are not geometric areas. A fixed nonempty set $\mathcal K\subseteq\mathcal T$ specifies the required inspection items before scenario evaluation. The camera attitude quaternion $q_k$ defines unit boresight $\mathbf b(q_k)$, and $\mathcal O$ denotes the occluding mesh. Target index $i$ is visible when
\begin{align}
  r_{\min}^{\mathrm{cam}}&\le\norm{\mathbf p_i-\mathbf r_k}\le r_{\max}^{\mathrm{cam}},\nonumber\\
  \arccos\!\frac{(\mathbf p_i-\mathbf r_k)^{\mathsf T}\mathbf b(q_k)}
  {\norm{\mathbf p_i-\mathbf r_k}}&\le\alpha_{\max},\nonumber\\
  \arccos\!\frac{(\mathbf r_k-\mathbf p_i)^{\mathsf T}\mathbf n_i}
  {\norm{\mathbf r_k-\mathbf p_i}\norm{\mathbf n_i}}&\le\theta_{\max},\nonumber\\
  \los(\mathbf r_k,\mathbf p_i,\mathcal O)&=1.
  \label{eq:visibility}
\end{align}
Here $r_{\min}^{\mathrm{cam}}$ and $r_{\max}^{\mathrm{cam}}$ are the camera range limits, $\alpha_{\max}$ is the FOV half-angle, $\theta_{\max}$ is the incidence-angle limit, and $\los(\mathbf r_k,\mathbf p_i,\mathcal O)=1$ denotes an unobstructed LOS. Let $\mathbf m_k\in\{0,1\}^{N}$ denote the cumulative covered-target mask after the first $k$ selected observations, initialized at $\mathbf m_0=\mathbf 0$. Let $M$ denote the number of candidate observation nodes, indexed by $j=1,\ldots,M$. For a target-index set $G_j$, let $\mathbf 1_{G_j}\in\{0,1\}^{N}$ denote its indicator vector; let $\mathbf e_a\in\{0,1\}^{M}$ denote the standard basis vector that marks selected destination $a$. Fig.~\ref{fig:sooa-framework}, panel~II, illustrates the range, FOV, incidence-angle, and LOS tests in \eqref{eq:visibility}. LOS is queried against a deterministic bounding-volume hierarchy containing every ISS mesh triangle; intersections at the target endpoint are excluded, while interior segment intersections block the view. In a closed-loop execution model, dwell could be accumulated as $\tau_{i,k+1}=\tau_{i,k}+\dt$ while target $i$ remains visible and accepted when $\tau_{i,k+1}\ge\tau_{\min}$, where $\tau_{i,k}$ is accumulated dwell time and $\tau_{\min}$ is the required dwell time. The offline study instead treats every candidate as a stabilized terminal observation with its prescribed exposure completed a priori. Applying \eqref{eq:visibility} at the candidate pose $(\mathbf r_k,q_k)=(\bar{\mathbf r}_j,\bar q_j)$ yields its fixed visibility mask $G_j$. Selecting node $j$ credits this mask, so $\mathbf m_{k+1}=\mathbf m_k\lor\mathbf 1_{G_j}$. Here $\mathbf m_k=[m_{1,k},\ldots,m_{N,k}]^{\mathsf T}$, where $m_{i,k}=1$ exactly when target/sample $i$ has been credited after the first $k$ observations.
Thus $\mathbf m_0=\mathbf 0$ is the initial all-uncovered mask, while $\mathbf m_k$ denotes
the generic mask at decision step $k$. The sampled-area coverage represented by mask $\mathbf m_k$ is
\begin{equation}
  C^{\mathrm{area}}(\mathbf m_k)=\frac{\sum_i a_i m_{i,k}}{\sum_i a_i}.
  \label{eq:coverage}
\end{equation}

Candidate generation can make a subset of the sampled surface invisible from every candidate observation. For the nonempty set $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{insp}}=\cup_{j=1}^{M}G_j$, we also report priority-weighted inspectable coverage:
\begin{equation}
  C_k^{\mathrm{insp}}=
  \frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{insp}}}w_i m_{i,k}}
  {\sum_{i\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{insp}}}w_i}.
  \label{eq:inspectable-coverage}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:coverage} estimates credited area over the original surface samples, whereas \eqref{eq:inspectable-coverage} is a priority-weighted score conditional on the candidate library. Their denominators remain fixed after scenario-specific observation loss. Neither is the primary required-target completion metric. Here, $\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf m)=\{i\in\mathcal T:m_i=1\}$ denotes the set of sample indices covered by binary mask $m$.
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  C^{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m)&=\frac{|\mathcal K\cap\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf m)|}{|\mathcal K|},\\
  \Gamma_{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m)&=\mathbf1\{\mathcal K\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf m)\}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:required-goal}
\end{equation}
Mission success requires $\Gamma_{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m)=1$, independently of optional area coverage. One observation can credit several required items, and different viewpoints can satisfy the same item. Completion denotes acceptance of the prescribed samples under \eqref{eq:visibility}. Reported mission duration is transfer-only; observation exposure is assumed complete at each stabilized candidate.

\subsection{Dynamic Feasibility and SOOAs}

The finite-body radius is $r_{\mathrm{veh}}=\unit{0.80}{m}$, which conservatively bounds the chaser visual model, and the prescribed mesh-clearance margin is $d_{\mathrm{safe}}=\unit{2.0}{m}$. For an edge rollout with $K$ post-step samples, let $\varepsilon_r$ and $\varepsilon_v$ denote the terminal position and velocity tolerances. Every sampled state must satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \norm{\mathbf u_k}_2\le u_{\max},\qquad
  \norm{\mathbf v_k}_2\le v_{\max},\qquad
  d_{\mathcal S}(\mathbf r_k)\ge 0,
  \label{eq:limits}
\end{equation}
where $d_{\mathcal S}(\mathbf r)=d_{\mathrm{mesh}}(\mathbf r)-r_{\mathrm{veh}}-d_{\mathrm{safe}}$ is finite-body clearance above the required margin, $d_{\mathrm{mesh}}$ is unsigned center distance to the closest triangle in the complete ISS mesh, and $d_{\mathcal S}=0$ is the sampled admissibility boundary. The mesh coordinates are obtained from the complete GL Transmission Format (glTF) scene hierarchy, followed by the Simulation Description Format (SDF) pose and scale. In addition, each consecutive pair of rollout samples is tested for a triangle-surface intersection, and its continuous center-distance lower bound subtracts half the segment length from the smaller endpoint distance by the 1-Lipschitz property of distance to a closed set. This swept test catches a direct mesh crossing even if both endpoints have positive unsigned distance. For a directed rollout from source node $i$ to destination node $j$, define $\Delta v_{ij}=\sum_k\norm{\mathbf u_{ij,k}}_2\dt$, root-mean-square distance $e_{ij}^{\mathrm{rms}}$ from the transfer samples to the destination position, and $d_{ij}^{\min}=\min_k d_{\mathcal S}(\mathbf r_{ij,k})$. The coefficients $\lambda_v$, $\lambda_e$, $\lambda_s$, and $\lambda_a$ are fixed nonnegative weights for maneuver increment, terminal tracking error, clearance violation, and per-edge action cost, respectively. The graph stage cost is
\begin{equation}
  \ell_{ij}=\lambda_v\Delta v_{ij}+\lambda_e e_{ij}^{\mathrm{rms}}+\lambda_s\max(0,-d_{ij}^{\min})+\lambda_a.
  \label{eq:edge-cost}
\end{equation}
For any route, $J$ denotes the sum of its edge stage costs; $J_{\mathrm{rollout}}$ in the illustrative figure is the corresponding realized rollout cost.
For every shield-admissible edge, the clearance-violation term is zero. It remains in the shared cost implementation so deliberately unshielded comparison methods can be evaluated with the same expression.

A candidate observation node and a directed SOOA edge are, respectively,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  c_j&=(\bar{\mathbf r}_j,\bar q_j,G_j),\\
  \mathsf a_{ij}&=\bigl(\mathbf x_{ij}[1{:}K],\mathbf u_{ij}[0{:}K-1],q_{ij}[1{:}K],\\
  &\qquad \Delta v_{ij},d_{ij}^{\min},u_{ij}^{\max},v_{ij}^{\max},e_{ij}^{r},e_{ij}^{v},\rho_{ij}\bigr).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:sooa}
\end{equation}
Here $\bar{\mathbf r}_j$ and $\bar q_j$ are the terminal observation pose and prescribed attitude, $G_j\subseteq\mathcal T$ is its camera-valid target set, and $e_{ij}^{r}$ and $e_{ij}^{v}$ are terminal position and velocity errors. The stored state and attitude arrays contain the $K$ post-step samples, while $u_{ij}^{\max}$ and $v_{ij}^{\max}$ record the maximum sampled input and speed norms; the source state is held by candidate node $c_i$. In the edge notation below, $i$ and $j$ index candidate nodes and are distinct from the surface-sample index used above. The initial condition is a distinguished source node $c_0$ with no visibility reward. For a passive audit with horizon $T_p=L_p\dt$, where $L_p$ is the number of passive steps, zero-input HCW drift can be propagated from every stored edge state and recorded as $\rho_{ij}=\min_{1\le k\le K,\,0\le l\le L_p}d_{\mathcal S}(\mathbf r_{ij,k}^{\mathrm{drift}}(l))$. The reported study disables this optional audit and therefore makes no passive-safety claim; it retains input, speed, full-mesh sampled clearance, swept surface-intersection, and terminal checks. Throughout this paper, ``safe'' means admissible under the audits explicitly enabled for the reported experiment.

The preceding model induces the required-target sequencing problem. The graph
state and shield notation used to solve it are introduced in Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}.
\begin{samepage}
\begin{problem}[Required-target SOOA planning]
\label{prob:required-target-sooa}
Given the candidate nodes and stored directed SOOA records above, an initial
action budget $H$, and the required set $\mathcal K$, find a finite sequence
$\pi=(a_1,\ldots,a_L)$, with $0\le L\le H$, that minimizes
\begin{equation}
  J(\pi)=\sum_{k=1}^{L}\ell_{j_{k-1}a_k},
  \qquad j_0=0,\quad j_k=a_k,
  \label{eq:problem-objective}
\end{equation}
subject to the following conditions: each traversed record
$\mathsf a_{j_{k-1}a_k}$ passes all enabled motion, terminal, clearance, and
passive audits; no destination is selected more than once; and the masks
updated by $\mathbf m_k=\mathbf m_{k-1}\lor\mathbf 1_{G_{a_k}}$, $\mathbf m_0=\mathbf0$, satisfy
$\Gamma_{\mathcal K}(m_L)=1$. A zero-gain action is allowed when it is needed
to reach a later required observation. If no such sequence exists within $H$,
the instance is declared infeasible in the available candidate graph.
\end{problem}
\end{samepage}

\section{Viability-Preserving Rollout ADP}
\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{Candidate Graph and Markov State}

Let $\mathcal V=\{c_0,c_1,\ldots,c_M\}$ contain the initial node and the $M$ candidate observation nodes in \eqref{eq:sooa}. A directed edge from $c_i$ to $c_j$ is the source-dependent SOOA $\mathsf a_{ij}$, and it is absent when an enabled audit fails. Thus visibility $G_j$ is node dependent, whereas trajectory, cost, clearance, input, terminal error, and passive margin are edge dependent. This separation is necessary because reaching the same observation from two different source nodes generally produces different HCW motion and audit values.

A node identifier is not a sufficient decision state because the value of a view changes after its targets have been covered. The finite-horizon Markov state is
\begin{equation}
  s_k=(j_k,\mathbf m_k,b_k,h_k),
  \label{eq:mdp-state}
\end{equation}
where $j_k\in\{0,\ldots,M\}$ is the current node, $\mathbf m_k\in\{0,1\}^{N}$ is the covered-target mask, $b_k\in\{0,1\}^{M}$ is the selected-observation mask, and $h_k$ is the remaining action budget. The scalar destination index $a$ is distinct from the surface-area scalar $a_i$ defined above. Selecting destination index $a\in\{1,\ldots,M\}$ traverses edge $\mathsf a_{j_ka}$ and gives the deterministic transition
\begin{equation}
  f(s_k,a)=\left(a,\;\mathbf m_k\lor\mathbf 1_{G_a},\;b_k\lor\mathbf e_a,\;h_k-1\right).
  \label{eq:mdp-transition}
\end{equation}
The required set $\mathcal K$ is fixed problem data. The shield-admissible action set $\mathcal U_s(s)$ is defined below. The constrained graph problem minimizes total stage cost subject to \eqref{eq:required-goal}, with zero terminal cost for a completed task and value $+\infty$ when no admissible completion exists within the remaining budget. Its Bellman recursion is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  V^\star(s)&=
    \begin{cases}
    0, & \Gamma_{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m)=1,\\
    +\infty, & h=0\ \text{or}\ \mathcal U_s(s)=\varnothing,\\
    \min\limits_{a\in\mathcal U_s(s)}
      Q^\star(s,a), & \text{otherwise},
    \end{cases}\\
  Q^\star(s,a)&=\ell_{j a}+V^\star(f(s,a)).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:bellman}
\end{equation}
Here $j$ and $h$ are the current-node and remaining-budget components of $s$, and $f$ is the deterministic state-transition map. Exact evaluation is exponential in the coverage and selected-node masks. The proposed method approximates this recursion with a short exact prefix and a computationally inexpensive, goal-aware terminal policy.

\subsection{SOOA Construction and Safety Shield}

Area-weighted targets are sampled from the mesh, and camera seeds are generated on offset shells around their outward normals. The binary visibility matrix $\mathsf V_{ij}=\mathbf 1\{i\in G_j\}$ is computed once from the terminal observation geometry. For a transition to $\bar{\mathbf x}_j=[\bar{\mathbf r}_j^{\mathsf T},\mathbf0^{\mathsf T}]^{\mathsf T}$, the planner solves the minimum-energy piecewise-constant HCW boundary-value problem under the numerical fourth-order Runge--Kutta (RK4) map
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \min_{\mathbf u_0,\ldots,\mathbf u_{K-1}}\quad&
  \sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\norm{\mathbf u_k}_2^2\\
  \mathrm{s.t.}\quad&
  \mathbf x_{k+1}=\widetilde A_d\mathbf x_k+\widetilde B_d\mathbf u_k,\\
  &\mathbf x_K=\bar{\mathbf x}_j.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:terminal-transfer}
\end{equation}
If the terminal influence matrix $\mathcal B_K=[\widetilde A_d^{K-1}\widetilde B_d\ \cdots\ \widetilde B_d]\in\mathbb R^{6\times3K}$ has full row rank, stack the commands as $\mathbf U_{ij}=[\mathbf u_{ij,0}^{\mathsf T},\ldots,\mathbf u_{ij,K-1}^{\mathsf T}]^{\mathsf T}$. The minimum-norm command from source state $\mathbf x_i^{\mathrm{src}}$ is then $\mathbf U_{ij}^\star=\mathcal B_K^{\mathsf T}(\mathcal B_K\mathcal B_K^{\mathsf T})^{-1}(\bar{\mathbf x}_j-\widetilde A_d^K\mathbf x_i^{\mathrm{src}})$. The implementation computes this expression through the $6\times6$ Gram system and then propagates the requested input without clipping. Let $\chi_{ij}=1$ exactly when every stored edge sample satisfies \eqref{eq:limits}, the enabled swept-segment audit reports no triangle intersection, $e_{ij}^{r}\le\varepsilon_r$, $e_{ij}^{v}\le\varepsilon_v$, and $\rho_{ij}\ge0$ when the passive audit is enabled. The shield then admits
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal U_s(s)=\{a\in\{1,\ldots,M\}:h>0,\;b_a=0,\;\chi_{ja}=1\},
  \label{eq:safe-action-set}
\end{equation}
where $j$ is the current-node component of $s$. Safe unvisited nodes remain admissible when they provide no immediate inspection gain, because a connecting action may be needed to reach a required target on the directed graph. The finite budget and no-revisit mask bound such connections. The shield makes an inadmissible edge unavailable to incumbent construction, local search, and exact recursion \cite{alshiekh2018shielding}. Its guarantee remains conditional on the sampled numerical rollout and deterministic model used to compute $\chi_{ij}$.

An edge is audit-admissible when $\chi_{ij}=1$; an action is shield-admissible when it belongs to $\mathcal U_s(s)$. A scenario is feasible for the candidate graph when at least one shield-admissible sequence reaches $\Gamma_{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m)=1$ within the available budget, and is infeasible when no such sequence exists.

\subsection{Adaptive Base Policy and Rollout Value}

Let $g_{\mathcal K}(s,a)=|\mathcal K\cap(G_a\setminus\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf m))|$ count newly accepted required targets, and let $\epsilon=0.05$ prevent division by a near-zero stage cost. For the required-target task, the deterministic adaptive base policy is
\begin{equation}
  \mu(s)\in\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal U_s(s)}
  \left(\frac{g_{\mathcal K}(s,a)}{\max\{\epsilon,\ell_{ja}\}},
  g_{\mathcal K}(s,a),-\ell_{ja}\right),
  \label{eq:base-policy}
\end{equation}
where the tuple is compared lexicographically, followed by deterministic node-identifier tie breaking. The policy recomputes gain after every accepted observation; zero-gain ties favor lower stage cost. Starting from state $s$, its completion value is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat V_0(s)=
  \begin{cases}
  \sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\ell_{j_k\mu(s_k)}, & \mu\text{ reaches }\Gamma_{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m)=1\text{ within }h,\\
  +\infty, & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:base-value}
\end{equation}
where $L$ is the number of actions taken by the base policy before completion or budget exhaustion. Thus $\widehat V_0$ approximates the cost-to-go through a complete, shielded base-policy simulation.

For rollout depth $d\ge1$, define the truncated Bellman value
\begin{equation}
  \widehat V_d(s)=
  \begin{cases}
    0, & \Gamma_{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m)=1,\\
    +\infty, & h=0,\\
    \min\limits_{a\in\mathcal U_s(s)}\widehat Q_d(s,a), & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:rollout-value}
\end{equation}
where $\widehat Q_d(s,a)=\ell_{ja}+\widehat V_{d-1}(f(s,a))$.
The minimum over an empty action set is $+\infty$. The policy selects a finite-valued minimizing action, applies \eqref{eq:mdp-transition}, and rebuilds the depth-$d$ tree at the successor state. State--depth memoization removes duplicate subproblems within each decision. The guarantees below require exhaustive prefix evaluation and the same deterministic Markov base policy at every leaf.
Fig.~\ref{fig:adp-architecture} gives a worked decision on an illustrative graph, separating motion admissibility from completion certification and showing the continuation retained after replanning.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Viability-preserving rollout ADP}
\label{alg:safe-graph-api}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Candidate graph, shield $\mathcal U_s$, required set $\mathcal K$, budget $H$, depth $d$
\State $s\gets(0,\mathbf0,\mathbf0,H)$; $\pi\gets()$ \Comment{$\pi$ stores the selected action sequence}
\While{$\Gamma_{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m)=0$ and $h>0$}
  \State Enumerate every $a\in\mathcal U_s(s)$
  \For{each admissible $a$}
    \State $\widehat Q_d(s,a)\gets\ell_{ja}+\widehat V_{d-1}(f(s,a))$
  \EndFor
  \State Discard every action with $\widehat Q_d(s,a)=+\infty$
  \If{no finite action remains} \State \Return no certified completion \EndIf
  \State $a^\star\gets\arg\min_a \widehat Q_d(s,a)$ with deterministic ties
  \State Append $a^\star$ and its audit record to $\pi$
  \State $s\gets f(s,a^\star)$
\EndWhile
\State \Return $\pi$ if $\Gamma_{\mathcal K}(\mathbf m)=1$; otherwise return no certified completion
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Properties and Exact Oracle}
\label{sec:theory}

The coverage result below records the standard diminishing-returns structure of weighted set coverage \cite{nemhauser1978submodular}. The subsequent analysis makes the SOOA state and audit assumptions explicit and specializes rollout feasibility and policy-improvement arguments \cite{bertsekas1997combinatorial,bertsekas2020rollout} to the required-target terminal predicate.

\begin{proposition}[Monotone submodularity of fixed-mask coverage]
\label{prop:submodular-coverage}
For an observation set $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,M\}$, let $F(S)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_i\mathbf1\{i\in\cup_{j\in S}G_j\}$. If all $w_i\ge0$, then $F$ is normalized, monotone, and submodular.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Clearly $F(\varnothing)=0$. If $A\subseteq B$, every target covered by $A$ is covered by $B$, so nonnegative weights give $F(A)\le F(B)$. For $a\notin B$, the marginal gain $F(A\cup\{a\})-F(A)$ is the total weight in $G_a\setminus\cup_{j\in A}G_j$. Since $A\subseteq B$, this set contains $G_a\setminus\cup_{j\in B}G_j$, proving diminishing returns.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{remark}[Role of submodularity]
Proposition~\ref{prop:submodular-coverage} also holds for required-target gain by assigning unit weight to $\mathcal K$ and zero elsewhere. It does not justify removing zero-gain connecting actions or supply a greedy approximation ratio for a directed inspection tour. Both SOOA cost and the admissibility of the next observation depend on the current node.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Markov sufficiency]
\label{prop:markov-sufficiency}
For a fixed deterministic candidate graph with fixed node masks, required set $\mathcal K$, source-dependent edge records, target weights, revisit rule, and horizon, the state in \eqref{eq:mdp-state} is Markov for the finite graph decision problem.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
The current node $j_k$ identifies the source of every possible next edge; $\mathbf m_k$ and fixed $\mathcal K$ determine the remaining inspection requirements; $b_k$ determines which observations remain selectable under the no-revisit rule; and $h_k$ determines the remaining budget. By assumption, visibility masks and edge costs and audits are deterministic records indexed only by the current and destination nodes. Equations~\eqref{eq:mdp-transition}, \eqref{eq:bellman}, and \eqref{eq:safe-action-set} therefore depend on no earlier history once $(j_k,\mathbf m_k,b_k,h_k)$ is given. The proposition concerns binary prescribed-observation acceptance; partial dwell, multiple independent look directions, or time-dependent illumination would require additional state.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{proposition}[Compositional preservation of stored audits]
\label{prop:audit-composition}
Consider a sequence $(\mathsf a_{j_{k-1}j_k})_{k=1}^{L}$ whose first source is $c_0$, whose idealized rest-to-rest endpoints match exactly, and whose every edge has $\chi_{j_{k-1}j_k}=1$. Then every stored sample of the concatenated sequence satisfies the input, speed, and clearance inequalities, every edge endpoint satisfies its terminal conditions, and every stored passive-drift rollout has nonnegative margin when that audit is enabled.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
By the definition of $\chi_{ij}$, all stated conditions hold on each accepted edge record. Exact endpoint matching identifies the terminal state of edge $k$ with the source state of edge $k+1$, so the records concatenate without an unmodeled jump. The sample set of the complete trajectory is the finite union of the per-edge sample sets; therefore every member of that union retains the corresponding per-edge audit result.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{remark}[Meaning of audit preservation]
The proposition is a finite-record composition result, not a continuous-time barrier certificate. It does not cover inter-sample clearance, endpoint perturbations beyond tolerance, closed-loop tracking error, or robustness to model and state uncertainty.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Rollout viability]
\label{prop:rollout-viability}
For the deterministic finite graph, if $\widehat V_d(s)<+\infty$, Algorithm~\ref{alg:safe-graph-api} selects a shield-admissible action whose successor has a finite goal-reaching completion within the remaining budget. Replanning preserves this property until the goal is reached.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Finiteness in \eqref{eq:rollout-value} provides a certificate consisting of shield-admissible prefix actions followed, unless the goal is reached earlier, by a base-policy completion. The selected first action is part of such a certificate, so its successor retains a goal-reaching sequence within the reduced budget. When the depth-$d$ tree is rebuilt, the certificate tail remains admissible; if another exact prefix action is required, the next action of the certified base completion supplies it. Determinism preserves the recorded masks, costs, and audits. Induction over the decreasing action budget therefore proves goal attainment.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{proposition}[Base-policy no-degradation]
\label{prop:base-bound}
If the adaptive base policy reaches the goal from $s$, then $\widehat V_d(s)$ is finite and $\widehat V_d(s)\le\widehat V_0(s)$ for every $d\ge1$. For a fixed depth $d$, the realized graph cost of repeated replanning in Algorithm~\ref{alg:safe-graph-api} is no greater than $\widehat V_d(s)$ and therefore no greater than the base-policy cost.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
The first $d$ base-policy actions, truncated if the goal is reached earlier, followed by the remaining base completion form a feasible certificate in \eqref{eq:rollout-value}; hence $\widehat V_d(s)\le\widehat V_0(s)$. Now take a minimizing depth-$d$ certificate at a replanning state and let $s'$ be the successor of its first action $a$. The certificate tail, extended when necessary by the next base-policy action, is feasible in the rebuilt depth-$d$ tree at $s'$. Therefore $\ell_{ja}+\widehat V_d(s')\le\widehat V_d(s)$. Summing this inequality over successive replans telescopes to a realized cost no greater than the initial $\widehat V_d(s)$. The result concerns the graph stage cost; reductions in physical $\Delta v$ are evaluated separately.
\end{IEEEproof}

For reduced-graph certification, exact recursion caches \eqref{eq:bellman} for every reachable tuple $(j,m,b,h)$ and enumerates every action in $\mathcal U_s(s)$ rather than a local reversal neighborhood.

\begin{theorem}[Optimality on an exhausted reduced SOOA graph]
\label{thm:reduced-optimality}
For fixed finite nodes, deterministic visibility masks, deterministic audited edge costs, a required set $\mathcal K$, the no-revisit rule, and horizon $H$, exhaustive evaluation of \eqref{eq:bellman} returns a minimum-cost task-completing shield-feasible sequence when one exists. If no such sequence exists, it returns value $+\infty$ and reports infeasibility.
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Proceed by induction on the remaining budget $h$. At $h=0$, \eqref{eq:bellman} returns zero exactly for a goal state and $+\infty$ otherwise, which is correct. Assume the recursion is correct for all states with at most $h-1$ remaining actions. At a state with $h$ actions, every feasible sequence begins with exactly one $a\in\mathcal U_s(s)$ and then a feasible continuation from $f(s,a)$; by the induction hypothesis, the recursion assigns that continuation its minimum cost or $+\infty$ if none exists. Minimizing over every admissible first action therefore returns the minimum total cost, or $+\infty$ when all continuations are infeasible. The claim is limited to the exhausted sampled graph and does not extend to omitted camera poses or continuous-space trajectories.
\end{IEEEproof}

With maximum branching factor $B_{\max}$, depth $d$, and at most $H$ base-policy actions, direct decision evaluation costs $O(HB_{\max}^{d+1})$ in the worst case. Memoization reduces repeated evaluation when prefixes reach the same mask state. Fig.~\ref{fig:adp-depth-sensitivity} and Table~\ref{tab:depth-sensitivity} quantify the offline cost--computation trade-off for $d=1,\ldots,6$.

\subsection{Matched Baselines and Statistical Reporting}

The confirmation includes the task-aware greedy incumbent, one-step ADP, ADP-seeded local search, and depth-three ADP under identical tasks, dynamics, safety gates, and action budgets. One-step ADP uses \eqref{eq:rollout-value} with $d=1$. Local search applies three audited best-improvement reversal passes to its completed route and retains the feasible seed if improvement fails. \subsection{All-Solvable Redundant-Viewpoint Benchmark}
\label{sec:redundant-view-protocol}

A subsequent pilot tested whether redundant viewpoints reduce the visibility bottleneck while permitting independent route certification. Three frozen seeds generated 33-view graphs on the same mesh, with the same nine required targets, initial state, camera model, dynamics, costs, and safety limits. Each graph contained three separated views per required target and six connecting views anchored to other observable samples. Candidate positions were sampled \unit{26--42}{m} from their source target within a $35^{\circ}$ cone around its surface normal, with the camera aimed at that target. From 64 attempts per source, deterministic first-valid and farthest-point selection retained geometry-valid views separated by at least \unit{4}{m}. All rejected attempts were archived. Each graph's $33+33\times32=1\,089$ directed transfers were regenerated and audited against the full mesh.

Each graph supplied one reference case, ten nominal cases and ten shifted cases, using the confirmation's node-removal probability ranges. All 63 cases were retained. The 59 cases with an independent feasible certificate define the primary all-solvable benchmark (three reference, 30 nominal and 26 shifted cases). The four remaining shifted cases are retained as a visibility-loss stress test and are excluded only from the primary solvable-case denominator. Background weights were fixed because they do not affect required-only completion or graph cost. The generation rules, seeds and source hashes were frozen before graph construction. This pilot followed inspection of the confirmation failures and is development evidence; it does not replace the frozen confirmation.

An independent mixed-integer linear program (MILP) selected a source-to-sink path over admissible edges. Binary arc and node variables enforced flow, required-target coverage, no revisits and the 14-observation budget; increasing node ranks excluded disconnected cycles. Its objective was the same additive graph cost, including the 0.05 action charge. A reconstructed valid route certified feasibility. Missing required visibility or a solver infeasibility result certified impossibility in the available graph; a limit without a valid route remained unresolved. Optimality was claimed only after solver proof and route-cost reconstruction. Certification preceded ADP evaluation and supplied no route to either ADP depth.

Greedy construction, one-step ADP, depth-three ADP and the independent MILP each received a nominal \unit{15}{s} budget, including initialization or model construction and excluding shared graph generation. The pilot used Python~3.12.3 and SciPy~1.11.4/HiGHS on Ubuntu. Each method--case pair was timed once; greedy/ADP order was shuffled, while MILP timing came from the preceding certification phase. Native solver termination could slightly exceed its requested limit. The primary success-rate denominator is the independently certified all-solvable benchmark; all 63 generated cases and the four stress cases remain available for transparent audit. Cost gaps used only cases with proven optima.

Execution success requires every observation to pass and every required target to be accepted; the supplemental hybrid case also requires 95\% weighted background coverage. \section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results support model-based rollout ADP for prescribed-target inspection. A base-policy completion supplies a checkable continuation, while Bellman lookahead improves the next action. The required-target constraint determines mission completion independently of optional background coverage. This sequencing layer complements learning-based proximity controllers \cite{hu2021learning,tian2024proximity} and run-time-assured DRL inspection \cite{vanwijk2024inspection}; the reported baseline comparisons evaluate sequencing performance, not superiority over those controllers.

The task formulation also accommodates a hybrid goal with an additional background threshold, provided the base policy and rollout boundary use the same predicate. Target priorities alone cannot impose mandatory observations. The coverage-only ablation illustrates this difference directly.

The primary comparison measures maneuver savings against one-step ADP and its seeded reversal-search baseline. The viewpoint pilot additionally supplies independent feasibility certificates and proven cost optima for a subset of cases. Its common time budget exposes a trade-off: depth-three ADP improves completion and mean cost relative to one-step ADP, while MILP returns slightly lower mean costs with longer median runtimes. Repeated timing, broader search neighborhoods and a larger frozen confirmation are needed to assess this trade-off beyond the pilot.

Viewpoint availability is a separate requirement from successful sequencing. The original low shifted completion rate arose from missing mandatory visibility, so additional lookahead could not recover those cases. The redundant-viewpoint pilot addresses this observed limitation without changing required target identities. Its higher feasible fraction supports further evaluation of viewpoint redundancy, although the new library and protocol prevent interpreting the two campaigns as a paired algorithm improvement.

\subsection{Scope and Limitations}

The primary evidence covers synthetic prescribed samples on one deterministic observation graph; the development pilot adds three sampled graphs on the same mesh. Completion certifies those samples under the camera model, rather than full-region imagery or defect detection. Independent MILP certification checks route optimization, while visibility and clearance retain the existing geometry implementation. Operational inspection requires verified component requirements, denser sampling, resolution and dwell acceptance, illumination, and independent geometry.

The finite-completion safeguard may fail to find a route when its base policy is unsuccessful, even if another feasible route exists. The base-cost theorem applies when the base policy completes. Exact recursion and proven MILP optima concern only the specified finite graph; a time-limited feasible MILP route is not an optimum. Timing and confidence intervals characterize the stated desktop implementation and scenario generator, with no cross-host timing comparison. The observed depth-four-to-six plateau does not establish global optimality, and the pilot's 0.96\% gap applies only to its 27 cases with proven optima.

The model assumptions in Table~\ref{tab:assumptions} exclude navigation uncertainty, actuator and attitude dynamics, and illumination effects. Safety remains conditional on the enabled deterministic audits, with passive-drift auditing disabled. The study evaluates model-based ADP; extending it to a learned critic or actor would require preserving a valid continuation and the relevant cost bound.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper developed viability-preserving rollout ADP for prescribed-target inspection over audited orbital arcs. A finite Bellman prefix and task-aware base-policy value preserve completion and provide a conditional graph-cost bound.

ADP completed \RTTestSuccess/\RTTestN{} test and \RTOodSuccess/\RTOodN{} shifted missions, covering every feasible case in that confirmation; the remaining cases lacked required visibility. On jointly completed test missions, it reduced mean $\Delta v$ by 11.05\% relative to seeded local search. A subsequent redundant-viewpoint development pilot achieved 30/30 nominal and 26/30 shifted completions, plus all three reference cases, matching independently certified feasibility. Its mean graph-cost gap was 0.96\% across 27 proven optima. These findings support completion-certified sequencing and motivate a larger, separately frozen evaluation of viewpoint redundancy on the prescribed inspection model.
